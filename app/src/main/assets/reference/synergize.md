# Synergize — the fat-loss checklist

Five levers that compound with the training rather than standing in for it. Four of them are
on the daily card; the fifth is the training itself, which is the rest of this app.

**1 — Eat natural, unprocessed food.**
Not a macro target and not a calorie count. The distinction the book draws is how far the food
is from the state it grew in, because processing is what removes the fibre and the water that
make a portion filling at the volume it arrives in.

**2 — Stay cool.**
A body kept slightly below comfortable spends energy reheating itself, continuously and
without any decision from you. Lower the thermostat, wear one layer fewer, and drink cold
water. This is the same mechanism as the cold exposure pillar, at lower intensity and all day
rather than in a session — which is why a cold-water tick logged there counts here.

**3 — Drink enough, and cold.**
Around three litres a day. The volume matters on its own; the temperature adds the thermic
cost above.

**4 — Sleep well, and cool.**
Covered by the sleep pillar: 7–9 hours, in a room at 67–70°F. Recovery from a set taken to
failure happens here, not in the gym, which is why a run of short nights shows up as a
stalling lift before it shows up as anything else.

**5 — Train with high intensity.**
One set per exercise to positive failure. This is the whole training pillar, so it is not
restated as something to tick — ticking it would double-count the session you already logged.

## Omega-3 to omega-6

Aim for roughly 1:1. A standard Western diet runs closer to 20:1, almost entirely through
grain-fed meat and processed food made with seed oils.

Moving the ratio is a matter of what the food ate rather than of supplements:

- **Grass-fed meat** instead of grain-fed. The animal's diet is where the ratio comes from.
- **Oily fish** — mackerel, sardines, salmon.
- **Leafy greens**, which carry omega-3 in small amounts but in quantity.
- **Fewer seed oils** — sunflower, corn, soybean. This is the lever with the most slack in it,
  because these arrive through processed food rather than through anything anyone chose to eat.

No target is tracked for this. It is a shopping decision, not a daily one, and a number that
cannot be measured at home should not be presented as one that can.
